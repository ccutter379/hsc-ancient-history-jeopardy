Final Jeopardy — Revolts in the empire: Egypt and Babylon
Key features and developments: revolts in the empire — Egypt, Babylon
wagerThe traditional account says Xerxes punished Babylon's revolt (c. 484 BC) by destroying the Esagila temple, diverting the Euphrates and melting down the golden statue of Marduk. Name ONE modern historian who disputes this, and ONE piece of evidence they cite.Amélie Kuhrt or Russel Granger. Evidence (any one): cuneiform tablets show the title 'King of Babylon' continued in use throughout his reign; there is no evidence the Euphrates was ever diverted; no evidence of permanent destruction of the temple or cult; the 'stolen' statue was probably a minor votive offering, not the main cult idol. (The revolt, led by Bel-shimanni and Shamas-eriba, was crushed by Megabyzus.)
